# CanadaHelps Data Scientist Interview Guide (Round 2)

## What the interview will probably look like

### 1. Introductions (5–10 minutes)

Each interviewer will introduce themselves.

They may ask:

- ~~Tell us about yourself.~~
- ~~Walk us through your background.~~
- ~~Why are you interested in CanadaHelps?~~
- ~~Why this role?~~

---

### 2. Experience Discussion (15–20 minutes)

This is likely the biggest section.

Expect questions like:

- ~~Tell us about a machine learning project you've worked on.~~
- ~~What's your favorite data science project?~~
- ~~How did you clean messy data?~~
- ~~Tell us about a challenging dataset.~~
- ~~How do you decide which model to use?~~
- ~~Have you worked with SQL?~~
- ~~Tell us about a dashboard you built.~~
- ~~Have you ever presented findings to non-technical stakeholders?~~

> **Note:** These aren't asking for textbook definitions—they're asking about your own experience and decision-making.

---

### 3. Light Technical Questions (10–15 minutes)

Since there's no coding test, expect conceptual questions.

#### Python

- Which libraries do you use?
- pandas vs NumPy
- How do you handle missing values?

#### SQL

- Explain a JOIN you've used.
- How would you find duplicate records?
- How do you aggregate data?

#### Machine Learning

- Difference between classification and regression
- What is overfitting?
- Cross-validation
- Precision vs Recall
- Random Forest vs XGBoost
- Feature engineering
- Clustering
- Recommendation systems (mentioned in the job posting)

#### Statistics

- Mean vs Median
- Confidence intervals
- Hypothesis testing
- A/B testing (listed as an asset)

> **Note:** Nothing in the job posting suggests they'll ask you to derive formulas or solve advanced math problems.

---

### 4. Problem-Solving Discussion (10 minutes)

This is very common.

**Example:**

> We want to identify donors who are likely to stop donating. How would you approach it?

They aren't expecting a perfect answer.

They want to hear a structured thought process.

A good approach would be:

1. Understand the business problem.
2. Identify available data.
3. Explore the data.
4. Engineer relevant features.
5. Build a model.
6. Evaluate it.
7. Explain how the results would be used.

Another example:

> Donations dropped by 15%. How would you investigate?

They want to see analytical thinking and a structured approach.

---

### 5. Teamwork & Communication (5–10 minutes)

The job description emphasizes:

- Collaboration
- Storytelling
- Curiosity
- Learning
- Communication

Expect questions like:

- Tell us about a disagreement with a teammate.
- Describe a time you explained technical results to a non-technical audience.
- Tell us about a mistake you made.
- How do you prioritize work?
- What do you do when you don't know something?

---

## They Will Probably Ask About Projects on Your Resume

Be prepared to discuss every project you've listed.

For each project, be ready to explain:

- The business problem
- The data
- Data preprocessing
- Feature engineering
- Model selection
- Evaluation metrics
- Challenges you faced
- Results achieved
- What you would improve if you revisited the project

> **Tip:** If your resume mentions a project, assume they'll ask about it.

---

## They May Ask Mission-Related Questions

Since this is a nonprofit organization, expect questions like:

- Why CanadaHelps?
- Why do you want to use data science in the nonprofit sector?
- What interests you about donor behavior?
- How would data science help charities?

You don't need nonprofit experience. They're looking for genuine interest in applying data science to create social impact.

---

## Questions You Should Ask Them

At the end of the interview, you can ask thoughtful questions such as:

- What types of data science projects would this role tackle in the first six months?
- What does success look like for someone in this role?
- What are the biggest analytical challenges the team is currently working on?
- How does the Data & Analytics team collaborate with Product and Marketing?
- What opportunities are there to learn new AI or machine learning techniques?

These questions demonstrate curiosity about the role and the team's work.

# What I Would NOT Expect

Based on the interview invitation, I would **not** expect:

- Live coding on a whiteboard
- LeetCode-style algorithm questions
- A timed SQL assessment
- A take-home assignment (they explicitly stated there isn't one)
